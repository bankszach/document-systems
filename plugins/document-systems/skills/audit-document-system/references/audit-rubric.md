# Document-system audit rubric

## Identity and status

- Is one immutable Expression envelope identifiable?
- Do its digest and content-derived identity agree?
- Are Work continuity and supplied lineage distinguished?
- Are generated views and receipts separate from the Expression?

## Authority

- What gives the document force?
- Who proposes, decides, executes, verifies, accepts, and maintains?
- Are delegation, jurisdiction, precedence, and disagreement handled?

## Semantic model

- Are actors, objects, states, and consequential terms defined?
- Do requirements name an actor and observable behavior?
- Do transitions name entry state, gate, evidence, owner, exit state, and
  failure route where consequential?

## Evidence

- Are fact, inference, proposal, forecast, and acceptance distinguished?
- Does each consequential claim identify supporting evidence and limitations?
- Does retrieved or displayed information retain provenance?

## Exceptions

- Is there a stop or containment rule?
- Is an owner and required authority named?
- Is closure evidence defined?
- Can an exception silently become the normal course?

## Verification and acceptance

- Is structural integrity distinguished from operational readiness?
- Is verification distinguished from intended-use validation?
- Is acceptance absent from the Expression and left to identified project
  authority?

## Change

- Can revision create a new Expression without erasing its parent?
- Are affected requirements, procedures, tests, training, and views traceable?
- Is current head or supersession left unclaimed unless repository authority
  supplies it?

## Finding classes

- `keep`: fit for its intended job.
- `clarify`: ambiguous semantics or authority.
- `relocate`: valid detail at the wrong disclosure level.
- `promote-to-code`: deterministic repeated rule better enforced mechanically.
- `delete`: superseded or behaviorally harmful content with no unique evidence.
- `needs-evidence`: plausible but not sufficiently supported.
